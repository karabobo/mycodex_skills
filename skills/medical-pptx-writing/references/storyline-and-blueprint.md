# Storyline and Slide Blueprint

## Storyline standard

Build a causal sequence rather than a table of contents or parallel selling points:

`clinical problem -> why current care is insufficient -> therapeutic rationale -> evidence test -> clinical meaning -> implementation`

The therapy should appear as the answer to an accepted clinical problem, not as the opening assertion.

## Chapter pattern

For each chapter define:

1. Clinical problem
2. Medical answer or hypothesis
3. Evidence that tests it
4. Clinical meaning
5. Question handed to the next chapter

Example handoffs:

- `充分启动免疫只是第一步，能否转化为更早、更深的肿瘤控制？`
- `一次缓解并不等于长期生存，这种作用能否持续？`
- `长期获益只有在风险可管理时才能落地，如何识别和处理毒性？`

## Storyline prompt

> Design a causally connected medical presentation storyline for the stated audience and objective. Start from the clinical problem, not product advantages. For each chapter provide the question, medical answer, evidence, clinical meaning, and handoff question. Explain why each chapter must follow the previous one. Remove parallel selling points, repetition, unsupported causal bridges, and chapters that do not advance the audience's reasoning.

## Storyline stress-test prompt

> Stress-test this storyline. For each chapter ask: What problem does it solve? Why must it appear here? Does its evidence answer its title? What new question does it create? Identify parallel modules, logical jumps, repeated claims, and product claims introduced before the clinical need is established. Reorder or rewrite transitions rather than merely polishing wording.

## Slide blueprint schema

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Slide purpose | One communication task |
| Title | Question or evidence-matched conclusion |
| Key claim | One primary claim |
| Evidence/visual | Exact study, chart, diagram, or table |
| Interpretation | What the evidence means and does not mean |
| Clinical meaning | Relevance to decision or understanding |
| Transition | Link from previous and question for next |
| Source | Claim-source locator |

## Blueprint prompt

> Convert the approved storyline into a slide blueprint. For every slide provide purpose, title, one key claim, evidence/visual, interpretation, clinical meaning, transition, and source. Keep one primary task per slide. Split overloaded pages, remove pages that do not move the story, and ensure title, evidence, interpretation, and takeaway express the same level of certainty.

## Medical copy prompt

> Write concise, presentation-ready medical copy from the approved blueprint. Preserve population, setting, key statistic, and necessary qualification. Use natural professional language, not academic paragraphs or slogans. Do not add conclusions absent from the source. Use qualified language for exploratory, real-world, or mechanistic evidence. Return title, body copy, takeaway, and citation text.
